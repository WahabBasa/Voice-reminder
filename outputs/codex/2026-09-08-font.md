**Recommendation: Newsreader 400 and 500 for display text; keep SF Pro for the interface.** Use Source Serif 4 as the fallback choice. No files were modified.

## 1. Tiimo’s type system

The reference combines an **editorial serif for personality** with a **quiet sans for tasks and controls**.

The display face appears to be an old-style/transitional serif: pronounced thick–thin contrast, substantial vertical strokes, fine connecting strokes, gently bracketed serifs, and rounded terminals. Its lowercase has a moderate x-height with noticeable ascenders; it feels spacious without being especially narrow. It is warmer and less mechanically sharp than a Didot-style fashion serif.

The screenshots show several distinct roles:

| Role | Observed treatment |
|---|---|
| App Store headlines | Very large serif; regular introductory words, heavier emphasis on phrases such as “daily tasks” |
| Testimonial | Large regular serif; expressive through scale rather than weight |
| In-app day/task heading | Medium-to-semibold serif, much smaller than the marketing headlines |
| Task names | Compact sans, approximately semibold |
| Times and descriptions | Regular sans, smaller and quieter |
| Group labels | Small uppercase sans with modest tracking |

The body face looks like a contemporary grotesk sans: relatively large x-height, restrained shapes, and practical numerals. **The images do not establish its exact identity—or that it is custom.** SF Pro and several commercial grotesks can look very similar at this resolution.

Likewise, precise point sizes and numerical weights cannot be recovered from these scaled phone mockups. The useful reference is the hierarchy: within the app, serif headings appear roughly 1.5–2 times the task-title size; marketing headlines operate at a separate, much larger scale.

It reads as premium and calm because the serif has room to breathe, boldness is selective, and operational text stays straightforward. Pastel backgrounds, generous spacing, and limited visual competition contribute as much as the font.

**Framing correction:** App Store artwork is not a reliable specification for in-app typography. Borrow its balance and restraint, not its headline proportions.

## 2. Why Fraunces fails here

The problem is this particular application of Fraunces, rather than an inherent problem with serif headings.

- **Weight:** “Settings” is a dense black shape. Its swollen curves and emphatic terminals make 600 feel heavier than the number suggests.
- **Character:** Fraunces deliberately brings expressive, soft, historical forms. Here that character reads more quirky and nostalgic than composed and editorial.
- **Size relative to layout:** The heading has plenty of surrounding space, so excessive size is not the main issue. Its *visual mass* dominates the otherwise neutral screen. Simply shrinking it would leave a smaller version of the same mismatch.
- **Paywall:** The regular plan names are less problematic. The 500 prices still carry strong, rounded forms; alongside the thick selected-card border, they make the cards feel weighty.
- **Pairing:** SF Pro is not a bad partner. However, emphatic serif headings plus numerous 600/700 sans labels produce several competing sources of emphasis.

The implementation matters. [fonts.ts](C:/Dev/VR/lib/fonts.ts:1) loads separate 400/500/600 assets. I inspected the installed 600 TTF: **it is static, with no `fvar` table.**

Fraunces’s upstream optical-size axis changes contrast, x-height, spacing, and width; its separate softness and wonk axes affect character. But neither the screenshot nor the export name establishes the settings baked into Remi’s asset. It would be unjustified to diagnose “WONK is enabled” as the cause. Changing React Native `fontSize` does not retune this static font’s optical size. [Fraunces design documentation](https://github.com/googlefonts/fraunces)

The call-site audit also reveals a wider requirement than “replace the headings”: the serif appears at **15–20** in secondary paywall text, proof cards, date numerals, and sheets, as well as **22–34** in headings. A delicate poster-only face would be a poor global replacement.

## 3. Display font pick + fallback + npm package + weights

### Pick: Newsreader

**Package:** `@expo-google-fonts/newsreader`  
**Load only:** `Newsreader_400Regular` and `Newsreader_500Medium`, upright.

Newsreader offers the best balance of editorial warmth, controlled contrast, and usefulness across Remi’s actual sizes. Its forms are less bulbous than Fraunces, and medium has enough presence for “Settings” without turning it into a logo. It was designed for on-screen reading, which makes its smaller uses more defensible than a purely decorative display face. [Production Type’s description](https://github.com/productiontype/Newsreader)

Preserve the existing API exactly:

| Existing token | Proposed family |
|---|---|
| `FONT_DISPLAY` | `Newsreader_500Medium` |
| `FONT_DISPLAY_REGULAR` | `Newsreader_400Regular` |
| `FONT_DISPLAY_MEDIUM` | `Newsreader_500Medium` |

Two tokens can intentionally resolve to the same family. **Three token names do not require three font files.** The required weight exports are available through the [Expo Newsreader package](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@expo-google-fonts/newsreader).

### Fallback: Source Serif 4

**Package:** `@expo-google-fonts/source-serif-4`  
**Load:** `SourceSerif4_400Regular` and `SourceSerif4_500Medium`, using the same token mapping.

Choose this if Newsreader feels too literary in the actual app. Source Serif 4 is more restrained and sturdy, though less evocative of the reference. This is an alternative selection, not an additional family to bundle. [Expo package](https://github.com/expo/google-fonts/tree/main/font-packages/source-serif-4)

## 4. Body sans decision

**Keep SF Pro.**

My visual assessment of the alternatives:

| Sans | Likely effect |
|---|---|
| Inter | Similar practical neutrality; little gain over SF Pro on iOS. |
| DM Sans | Would add some roundness and warmth; the most plausible move toward the reference’s friendly tone. |
| Manrope | More geometric character; risks drawing attention to the interface text. |
| Plus Jakarta Sans | Broader, more overtly geometric personality; can make dense rows feel less compact. |

A bundled sans is not worth introducing for this change. The issue is display character and emphasis, not insufficient branding in body text. There is no established custom Tiimo sans to match.

The cost includes additional font assets, weight management, changed wrapping, and another loading dependency. Exact OTA size depends on the assets included in the export, not the package’s npm unpacked size; I would not invent a kilobyte estimate without measuring it.

## 6. iOS gotchas

- **Keep runtime asset loading.** Import the packaged font assets into `useFonts` in `lib/fonts.ts`. This fits the existing JS/asset OTA path; no config plugin or native rebuild is needed for the font substitution. [Expo font-loading guide](https://docs.expo.dev/develop/user-interface/fonts/)

- **Variable upstream does not mean variable installed asset.** Prefer explicit static weight exports. Do not assume automatic optical sizing or dependable arbitrary-axis control. Expo recommends static fonts for full platform support. [Expo variable-font guidance](https://docs.expo.dev/develop/user-interface/fonts/#variable-fonts)

- **Select the custom weight through its loaded family alias.** Use `Newsreader_500Medium` directly and omit additional `fontWeight` on that text. “iOS always ignores custom `fontWeight`” is too absolute: behavior depends on registration and family metadata. With separately registered runtime aliases, switching aliases is the predictable approach; a weight declaration does not load another asset. [Expo examples](https://docs.expo.dev/develop/user-interface/fonts/), [React Native weight behavior](https://reactnative.dev/docs/text-style-props#fontweight)

- **Tracking is not CSS `em`.** `letterSpacing: -0.3` is a logical-unit adjustment, not −0.3 em or a percentage. Keep negative tracking subtle, particularly at smaller sizes. [React Native text styles](https://reactnative.dev/docs/text-style-props#letterspacing)

- **Retain Dynamic Type scaling.** React Native text allows accessibility font scaling by default. Custom fonts still need layouts that accommodate larger text; do not disable scaling to preserve these screenshots. [React Native Text](https://reactnative.dev/docs/text#allowfontscaling)

- **Remi already has another scaling layer.** [scaleFontSize](C:/Dev/VR/lib/theme.ts:104) scales by device width up to 1.25×; it does **not** multiply by accessibility font scale. That is not currently double-counting Dynamic Type. Nevertheless, wider-device scaling plus accessibility sizing can substantially enlarge text. Check wrapping, card heights, and localized prices; allow stacked pricing cards at large sizes rather than shrinking essential text indefinitely.

This recommendation is grounded in the screenshots and source audit; the replacement has not been rendered on-device.
